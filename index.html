About

I develop statistical and machine learning methods that turn complex system data into timely, reliable decisions. Modern manufacturing and engineered systems collect far more data than they can use, so the central question in my work is not how to collect more data, but how to convert the data we have into quality improvement efficiently, when sensing, expert labeling, and experimentation are all limited.

My research interests include advanced manufacturing systems, statistical process monitoring and control, fault detection and diagnosis, active learning, reinforcement learning, and large language model agents.

Research

Measurement-efficient monitoringAdaptive sampling that decides where and when to measure for real-time anomaly detection.
Label-efficient diagnosisActive learning that directs scarce expert labels to the observations that matter most.
Experiment-efficient controlResidual reinforcement learning that builds on engineering knowledge for run-to-run control.

Adaptive sampling for monitoring

I developed adaptive sampling strategies that combine predictive modeling with sequential decision-making, both for mobile sensing vehicles scanning a spatial field and for partially observed networks. Applications include wildfire detection, the IEEE 118-bus power grid, and the Wabash Heartland Innovation Network.

Active learning for fault detection and diagnosis

I developed an online active learning framework that allocates a limited labeling budget between recognizing known fault types and discovering new ones, validated on steel-plate and robot-execution fault diagnosis. This work began during my research visit to Georgia Tech ISyE with Dr. Xiaochen Xian.

Residual reinforcement learning for process control

I developed a specification-aware residual reinforcement learning framework for run-to-run control that treats existing engineering controllers as a strong starting point, demonstrated on deep reactive ion etching and chemical mechanical planarization processes in semiconductor manufacturing.

What’s next

In the near term, I am developing self-improving AI agents for root cause analysis that ground their reasoning in quantitative process evidence, verify each hypothesized root cause before reporting it, defer to engineers when evidence is insufficient, and learn from their own failures. In the long term, I aim to extend these agents into a framework for decision-efficient closed-loop quality improvement that integrates monitoring, diagnosis, and control while keeping engineers in the loop.
